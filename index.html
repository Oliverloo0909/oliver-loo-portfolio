Levanta

Live on the App Store

Solo. Product, design, mobile, backend.

Logging a workout is the thing people quit over. So I made it one line: type "bench 3×8 80kg, rows 4×10" and it becomes structured sets. Ask the coach for a program and you get a real multi-week plan back.

The hard question with any AI product is what happens when it's wrong. My answer was a rule I don't break: the model never touches your data on its own. Every action it proposes is a widget you edit and confirm. That's the difference between a demo people try once and something someone trusts with a year of training history.

funhop

Live

Solo. Full stack and the whole data pipeline.

Deciding where to go out is a research project nobody wants. funhop takes your vibe, area, budget and group size, and hands back a two or three stop night built only from things genuinely happening that evening. No stale listings, no dead links.

Keeping that promise is the whole engineering problem. Seven stages of Python pull from Telegram and event sites, and an LLM turns unstructured posts into events you can actually filter on. Then 85% of the table went quietly wrong: no errors, no alerts, just images and links that had stopped resolving. Signed image URLs were losing their query strings, and I'd been synthesising detail URLs from title slugs that had 404'd for seven weeks. I now treat silent data rot as the default failure mode and encode every fix as a rule the pipeline enforces, because a recommender that quietly lies is worse than one that returns nothing.

Oui Chef! and Sushi Senpai

Langfun · in development

Solo studio. Engine, game, content, art direction.

Language apps lose people because practice feels like homework. So the lesson is a game you already understand in three seconds: run a Paris boulangerie or a Tokyo sushi bar, tap the right phrase before it slides off the belt. There's no tutorial because it doesn't need one.

Underneath the arcade layer it's a spaced-repetition scheduler, and the wrong answers are chosen to look plausible so you can't win by matching shapes instead of learning. I built it as one engine serving both titles, with language isolated to content files, so a third game is new phrases and new art rather than a rewrite.
